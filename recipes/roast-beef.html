Roast Beef

A Delicious Roast Beef -- (magnific.com)

Roast beef is a classic dish made from a relatively large cut of beef, typically from the sirloin, rib, or top round, that has been seasoned with salt, pepper, and often herbs like rosemary or thyme, then roasted in an oven at a high temperature to create a flavorful, browned crust while keeping the interior tender and juicy, usually served medium-rare to medium

Carved into thin slices, it is a centerpiece of traditional Sunday roasts in the UK, often accompanied by Yorkshire pudding, roasted potatoes, vegetables, and gravy, and is also popular cold in sandwiches, notably the French dip or the classic American roast beef sandwich.

Ingredients

  • Beef roast (such as ribeye, prim rib or top round)
  • Salt
  • Black pepper
  • Olive oil or Vegetable oil
  • Garlic
  • Fresh rosemary and thyme (you can use dried)
  • Paprika
  • Beef broth or stock
  • Butter, Onion, carrots, celery, or red wine for extra flavor (Optional)

Steps

  1. Remove the beef from the refrigerator and let it sit at room temperature for about 30–60 minutes (to ensure even cooking).
  2. Preheat your oven to a high temperature, typically 240°C (475°F) or 220°C (425°F) depending on the cut.
  3. Pat the beef dry thoroughly with paper towels (this helps achieve a good crust).
  4. Rub the roast all over with olive oil, then generously season with salt, black pepper, and any desired herbs or garlic.
  5. Sear the beef (optional but recommended): Heat a heavy pan or skillet over high heat and brown the roast on all sides, about 2–3 minutes per side.
  6. Place the roast on a rack inside a roasting pan (or directly in the pan) with the fat side up.
  7. Roast at high temperature for 15–20 minutes to create a browned crust.
  8. Reduce the oven temperature to around 160–180°C (325–350°F) and continue roasting until the desired internal temperature is reached (e.g., 52°C/125°F for medium-rare).
  9. Use a meat thermometer to check the temperature in the thickest part of the roast without touching bone or fat.
  10. Remove the roast from the oven when it is about 3–5°C (5–10°F) below your target temperature, as it will continue cooking while resting.
  11. Transfer the beef to a cutting board and loosely tent it with aluminum foil.
  12. Let the roast rest for 15–30 minutes (this allows juices to redistribute).
  13. Slice the roast beef thinly against the grain using a sharp carving knife.
  14. Serve warm with pan juices or gravy, or let cool for sandwiches.
